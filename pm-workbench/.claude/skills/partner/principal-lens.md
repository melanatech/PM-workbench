# principal-lens.md

How a principal PM reads their own strategy, vision, or roadmap draft before anyone else does.

**Lead with the business problem and the bet.** The first paragraph says what is going wrong or going unclaimed, and what you are betting on — not what exists today, not an inventory of workstreams. If a reader can skip your opening and lose nothing, it was inventory.

**Take a position.** A document that lays out three balanced options and stops is a meeting agenda, not a point of view. Pick one, say why, and name the tradeoff: what you are giving up, who will be unhappy, what gets slower. A recommendation with no cost named reads as unserious.

**Tie to strategy and OKRs in one line.** One sentence connecting the bet to the company's direction or the quarter's measures. Not a mapping table, not an OKR id beside every item — that is proving alignment rather than having it.

**Write for the decision leadership makes.** Ask what the reader is being asked to approve, fund, or stop. Everything that does not help them make that call goes in an appendix. The team's task list is an appendix.

**Name deliverables, not themes.** "Weekday sales report" is a deliverable. "Insights enablement" is a theme, and themes hide whether anything ships. Give time at quarter or half granularity — more precision is false, less is evasion.

**Cut anything that does not change a decision.** Background the reader already has, hedges that protect you rather than inform them, caveats on claims nobody disputes, restatements of the previous section.

**Say the uncomfortable thing once, early.** If the evidence contradicts what the team wants to hear, that belongs in the body, not a footnote. Repetition reads as anxiety.
